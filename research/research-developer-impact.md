# Developer Impact! - Jake Lenny


## Why Should Developers Care?
If You installed / Initial any non-explicitly trusted IDE extension, Installed AI Powered simply used NPM (the largest and default CI/CD) and downloaded a package with malicious code disguised as legitimate, or even were just unlucky enough to be actively using a new compromised IDE Extension (Nx Console Breach) Your SSH Keys, GitHub Credentials, the security and privacy of your Projects and API's (including any saved private information or tokens) may have been stolen, logged, or just messed with...

The 2026 Supply side attacks harmed developers and companies of all sizes and attacked the trust in GitHub security and livelihood regardless of size. over 30,000 GitHub repositories were attacked and compromised, including 4,000 breached in the Nx Console Trojan incident alone

## How was Developers trust in GitHub and NPM's security affected?

## Dependency Management
The key failing part of the attacks was not observing precaution when installing extensions and how you allowed to look through and access your code and IDE, in the case of the Nx Console Breach and other compromised extensions (which caused a ripple effect of other extensions getting compromised before GitHub and NPM brought the anti-malware hammer down) 

### Monitor Automatic Updates, Pushes to extensions 
You cant be idealistic with your safety, even lively updated and reputational extensions can be compromised. its within your best intentions to read up on and never automatically accept changes to your extensions and dependencies for your projects

### Give out the least Access possible to your dependencies
It may seem common sense to not give unrelated or total privileges to your extensions, but making sure everything is not over reaching and in its right place is beyond critical
-- **This Includes AI-Management apps, outside and semi related apps on your computer that may ask for access to private files / your GitHub, even if its not something you add / update for your IDE or project files, it might slither its way in there anyway if you give it permission**--

## MFA - Multi Factor Authentication & Token / SSH Security
Multi Factor Authentication is An Increasingly common but always critical safety feature, the more of your privacy and accessibility to your account, keys and API logins you can put behind directly needing your permission from a second device or passkey the better.

On the other-hand, the MOST Important safety steps you can take, is to not write your SSH Keys or API information anywhere someone can access with just reading your GitHub.
